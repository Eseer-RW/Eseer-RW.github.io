My role

I joined this project in September 2026 as an undergraduate research assistant, working with Prof. Daniel L. Chen (Toulouse School of Economics) and collaborators at CUNY Baruch, Columbia, and Chicago Booth. The published paper this page describes is by Lu, Dhanda, Chen, and Hansen — I am not an author on it. My work is the extension: reproducing the published results, then developing the multi-agent experiments that follow from them.

The problem

Off-the-shelf LLM agents behave poorly in strategic settings: they over-cooperate and barely respond to incentives. That is a problem now that agents are being deployed to set prices, negotiate, and trade. Standard alignment methods like RLHF were designed for single-agent, assistant-style interaction, and they do not encode the payoff structures that actually govern behavior in markets.

The approach

This project treats alignment as an objective-design problem instead of a post-training correction: specify the agent’s utility function up front, using preference structures from economics, and fine-tune on optimal play derived from theory. The training labels are synthetic and theory-derived rather than human-annotated.

Two specifications are compared:

  • Homo economicus — standard self-interest.
  • Homo moralis — Alger and Weibull’s Kantian universalizability, which partly weights the payoff an agent would receive if its opponent adopted its own strategy.

What I am extending

I joined in September 2026 to extend the work: first reproducing the published duopoly-pricing results, then developing multi-agent experiments on how preference-aligned agents affect prices, consumer welfare, and tacit algorithmic collusion. The immediate build is a set of repeated-pricing experiments that test whether preference-aligned agents converge to competitive or collusive prices — a live antitrust question wherever pricing is handed to an algorithm.

The stack is Python, PyTorch and HuggingFace, the OpenAI fine-tuning API, and oTree for the experiments. This work is in progress, and there are no results of my own to report yet.

The paper this builds on

Lu, Dhanda, Chen, and Hansen, Aligning AI Agents with Economic Preferences in Strategic Environments, arXiv:2507.20796. arxiv.org/abs/2507.20796
