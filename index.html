a little about me

i’m a first-year at harvard working through math 55 and the rest of the mathematics track. most of what i enjoy sits where proofs meet messy real data: i like the parts of a problem where the theory is clean and the dataset absolutely is not.

at insilicom i work on citation integrity — building an AI system that reads scientific references and decides whether they hold up. it turns out a surprising number don’t. i’ve since run that check across 54.7 million citations, which is the kind of sentence i did not expect to write as a freshman.

alongside that: a policy fellowship thinking about what autonomous AI does to market stability, an LLM translation benchmark for humanitarian crisis reports, and a slow, ongoing effort to actually understand quantitative trading rather than just read about it.

away from the screen — foil fencing (i referee too), first violin in the new york all-state symphonic orchestra twice over, and latin, which i’ve never quite been able to put down.

things i’ve built

a few projects i’m happy to talk about at length.

citation intelligence

an AI system that reads a paper’s references and decides whether they actually say what they’re cited for — vector search for retrieval, an LLM for the reasoning. 99% precision at 88% recall on a 44,000-case benchmark.

  • python
  • vector search
  • llm reasoning

citation fabrication at scale

an end-to-end study across 54.7M citations in 1.02M papers, surfacing ~2.6M (4.8%) references nothing can verify. found 41,000+ corrupted entries in a live 492M-record scholarly database and shipped the pipeline that cleans them.

  • crossref
  • openalex
  • data pipelines

chichewa translation benchmark

a benchmarking tool comparing 7 machine-translation systems over 778K+ chichewa–english sentence pairs, so crisis reports can reach english-speaking responders. built for ushahidi through harvard’s tech for social good.

  • bleu / chrf++
  • bertscore
  • comet

quant practice tool

my sandbox for learning systematic trading properly — data in, signal out, and a backtester built to be honest with me about what would actually have happened.

the path so far

where i’ve been studying and working.

  1. 2025 — 2029

    a.b. mathematics · harvard university

    math 55a and 55b (group theory, linear algebra, real and complex analysis, abstract algebra), cs50 and cs1240 (data structures, algorithm design and analysis), probability and statistics for engineering and data science, and a couple of psychology courses for the fun of it.

  2. 2025 — now

    software engineering intern · insilicom

    citation intelligence: an AI system detecting false and inaccurate references at 99% precision and 88% recall. ran the fabrication study across 54.7M citations, and built the automated data-quality pipeline that cleaned 41,000+ corrupted records out of a live 492M-record database. woburn, ma

  3. 2025 — now

    software engineer · harvard computer society, tech for social good

    built the LLM benchmarking tool evaluating 7 translation systems on 778K+ chichewa–english pairs for ushahidi, scored with bleu, chrf++, bertscore and comet, with batched parallel inference to keep runtimes sane. cambridge, ma

  4. 2025

    fellow · central market strategies

    authored a policy memorandum on AI’s long-term implications for market stability, algorithmic trading, and crypto markets — reading 30+ sources across federal legislation, SEC/CFTC/NAIC action, litigation, and think-tank research to weigh circuit breakers against autonomous AI shocks, herding risk from correlated training, and liquidation cascades in 24/7 leveraged venues. washington, dc

  5. 2025

    wise program · jane street

    probability and game theory applied in quantitative trading simulation workshops. new york, ny

  6. 2021 — 2025
